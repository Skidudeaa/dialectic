## The finding that started it

The Round fires Sunday 2026-08-23 and **nobody had ever seen a question it
writes**. So the real drafting path was run against all four qualifying rooms
with `today` pinned to that Sunday, posting nothing.

The form was right — binary, named source, hard close date, the GJP contract
intact. **The content was not.** Every question could have been written by a
stranger reading the news, which is precisely what the job's own prompt calls a
wasted slot. The drafter saw the room NAME, the thesis TITLE, and eight reading
summaries. It did not see the 15–19 causal-DAG node states, the desk's own
scenario probabilities, or a single thing either of them had said — all of it
already sitting in `rooms.trading_config` and `messages`.

Fixed. Re-run against Iran/Hormuz, the round now asks whether the 30-year yield
closes above 5.20% **and says why: it settles the room's own dispute about
whether the auction move was curve mechanics or the term-premium node firing.**
That dispute is a message in the transcript.

## The three capabilities

**The second slider.** Beside your own probability, where you think the other
one will land. Same append-only row, so the read is time-weighted for free. At
reveal the card says which way you misread them — signed, because reading them
low every week is a habit worth naming. The only number here that scores the
moment you both commit; it never waits on the world.

**Claude on the board.** The participant forecasts every round question itself,
with the real 20-tool registry live, under the same seal and the same rule, and
can be publicly and permanently wrong. De-risked end to end before a line was
written — the real loop against the live Japan room returned `iterations=3`,
`degraded=False`, and `PROBABILITY: 0.72`, having noticed unprompted that the
desk's own snapshot was stale.

**The settlement, and the Mirror.** A closed question now gets evidence
gathered from its own named source and one card asking a human for the verdict
— then a flat credit line naming who called it, at what number, on what date.
And the Mirror: 134 versions of each of them, written by the participant since
February, never read by the person they are about.

## Contracts a future session must know

All in `dialectic/CLAUDE.md`'s 2026-08-21 amendment. The five that bite hardest:

1. **`scheduler._tick` runs jobs SERIALLY.** A long job blocks every other job.
   This is why the house forecast is a bounded sweep, not an inline call.
2. **`stakes/house.py` is the single actor predicate.** `commitment_confidence.
   user_id` is nullable, so a house row in the humans' partition unseals the
   card. Use `split_by_actor` before anything else.
3. **The scoring window opens at the question's `created_at`**, not the first
   forecast. Landing that after the first settlement would have created two
   scoring eras in one table.
4. **The settlement's done-set is excluded in SQL, before the LIMIT.** THE LAW
   forbids the job resolving anything, so carded questions accumulate forever
   and a post-LIMIT filter starves the sweep silently.
5. **The Mirror's fence is the KEY, in the query.** Never a post-fetch filter.

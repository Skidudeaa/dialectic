# Dialectic — The Third Chair (moonshot, 2026-10-27)

*Written 2026-10-27 against the owner's brief: a collaborative space for Dan
and Amo to bounce ideas across many fields, an environment that makes them feel
closer than they are, and a participant with exquisite tools for running code
and acting on the web. Assumes twelve months and unlimited hands. Sits beside
`VISION.md` and `WORLD_LENS_VISION.md`; amends nothing silently.*

## Grounding (what is true in the tree today)

- The registry is **25 tools**, every one a read or a proposal
  (`tests/test_tools_registry.py` asserts 25). No code execution. No general
  web search (`read_article` and Reddit only). No voice. Presence is an
  `user_presence` row plus typing.
- `VISION.md` records "it does not take external actions" and "order placement
  remains categorically out", and names "graduated standing permissions" as the
  long arc.
- Graveyard corpses not re-proposed here: protocol-as-system-message; a judge
  panel of three same-model judges (correlated errors).
- The September enjoyment experiment measured human messages per day because
  volume was low. Its outcome is not re-derived here; see the memory note.

## Three premises to attack first

**"Virtual environment" does not mean a 3D world.** Second Life, Horizon
Worlds, Teamflow and Gather died the same death: embodiment costs friction, and
a world with two inhabitants is empty 95% of the time. The closeness
technologies that survived are always-on voice (Discord), shared cursors
(Figma) and the long-distance touch lamp (Filimin). Closeness is low latency
plus ambient awareness plus ritual. The World Lens already supplies all the
geometry needed.

**"Running code and interacting on the web" repeals a founding principle.**
Hands mean amending "no external actions" on the record, with a permission
ledger that two people hold keys to. The vision's own long arc anticipates
this. It must be a stated decision, not a drift.

**The limiter is pull, not features.** Hypothesis: nothing in a room does
something the pair could not do in a private Claude chat, so there is no reason
to go there instead. Every item below is judged by one question: does it make
the two of them open the house when nobody asked?

## The moonshot: three organs

### Organ 1 — Hands: the Workshop

A persistent Linux workspace per room. Firecracker microVMs if `/dev/kvm` is
present, otherwise bubblewrap plus a per-room unix user on a second box. Never
Docker. The workspace is a git repo: every run is a commit, every seed is
printed, every artifact (plot, CSV, notebook, script, PDF) becomes a workspace
object through the adapter pattern in `workspace_objects.py`, citeable like a
reading.

- `run_code` / `run_notebook`: streamed output lives in the room as one
  updating object, not a flood of messages.
- `start_job`: hours-long work (crawl, backtest, simulate, write) riding the
  existing `scheduled_job_runs` ledger (double-fire-proof, visible in the
  Console tray). It wakes with results; the morning brief cites them.
- A browser with a body: persistent Chrome profile per room, credentials from a
  vault, rrweb-style recording of every session. `browse`, `act_on_page`,
  `watch_page` (diff any URL over time, as `wire_watch` does for feeds). Humans
  scrub through what it did; that is how trust is built for a thing that acts.
- Search that exists: a provenance-stamped search tool (Exa / Brave / Kagi)
  feeding the reading queue, so `read_article` stops depending on pasted links.
- A registry that grows: `propose_tool` writes a Python function, a schema and
  a test inside the sandbox; both humans approve; it hot-loads into
  `build_registry`. The "25" assertion becomes a ledger. tradingDesk was the
  hand-built first organ; a biology room grows PubMed and a sequence viewer, a
  hardware room grows a firmware flasher, a writing room grows a manuscript
  differ, and the participant grows them overnight.
- The permission ledger: a `capabilities` table with scope, grantors, expiry,
  spend cap and revocation. Anything that spends money or posts outside the
  house needs both keys. Every action writes an event carrying the capability
  id. One kill switch on the Console. "Graduated standing permissions" as a
  table instead of a sentence.
- The real-money sleeve: `propose_trade` exists. After the paper book shows
  calibrated edge for a fixed number of months, a capped sleeve opens under two
  keys. Order placement moves from "categorically out" to "two-key, capped,
  replayed".

**Kill criterion.** Twelve weeks after `run_code`, `browse` and search land, if
human messages/day and artifacts-cited-by-humans have not moved, agency was not
the limiter. Pivot the budget to the Hearth.

### Organ 2 — Presence: the Hearth

The room is always on.

- Voice, always available: self-hosted LiveKit (a Go binary) per room, open mic
  or push-to-talk. Streaming transcription with diarization lands in the Record
  with speaker attribution (the memory speaker lane already understands it).
  The participant speaks through an ElevenLabs voice both humans choose, but
  only after raising a visible "wants to speak" glyph; the FSM decides when the
  hand goes up. It never talks over them.
- Voice notes as messages: thirty seconds, transcript beside audio. The other
  one hears the laugh.
- The lamp: not a green dot. A persistent signal of who is in the house, which
  room, what they are reading, for how long, plus opt-in off-app signals (local
  time, weather at their geo scope, calendar busy). Then physical: an ESP32
  with an LED ring and a small e-ink panel on each desk. Dan's lamp glows when
  Amo is in the house. A tap is a knock; the other device pulses; the
  participant says who knocked. Forty dollars of hardware.
- Multiplayer reading and Surface: Yjs on the Surface canvas and on reading
  highlights. Each sees the other's selection live. The participant notices
  when both mark the same paragraph.
- Follow mode: one drives the Bench, the World or the Library; the other
  follows; the participant narrates on request.
- A daily ritual: the Round is weekly. Add the porch, ten minutes at a fixed
  time, voice, with the participant running an agenda from open commitments and
  settling yesterday's claims. Distant friendships survive on rituals; the app
  should own one.

**Kill criterion.** Under three hours/week of co-present voice after eight
weeks means synchronous is not wanted; the budget moves to voice notes and the
lamp.

### Organ 3 — Character: the Third Chair

The participant stops being request-response.

- Continuous: an always-running agent loop with a daily token budget and its
  own desk, a room only it writes in and the humans can read. It works its
  agenda there (open questions, unsettled claims, promised follow-ups). The
  dev-session tools already read Claude Code sessions; turn the camera on the
  participant itself.
- The understudy: when one human is absent, the participant can argue the
  absent one's position from his memory lane and positions ledger, labelled
  "Dan (understudy)" on every line. Dan reviews on return and marks "that's not
  me", which corrects his model. Possible only because memory carries
  attribution. Also the riskiest item on this page; the label and the review
  loop are the only reasons it is allowed.
- The wiki: per room, a compiled, maintained document of current beliefs, open
  questions and decisions, with citations into the Record. The Record is the
  log; the wiki is the belief. Supersession made readable; over years, the
  second brain the vision promises.
- Skin in the game: the participant's Brier score goes on the House wall next
  to the humans', and it argues for research budget on that record.
- A council with mixed models: Steelman and Devil's Advocate run as internal
  sub-voices on different model families before the participant speaks on a
  contested claim; the dissent is shown. Same-model judges are a corpse.
- A voice and a glyph, not an avatar: the signature marks animate with FSM
  state (listening, thinking, working, idle).
- Time travel: event sourcing makes the house replayable to any date. "What
  did we believe on March 3, and what would you have said then with what you
  know now?" A counterfactual review of the pair's own reasoning; the log is
  already paid for.

## Sequencing and the first dollar

Fund the Workshop first, because pull: hands make a room worth entering over a
private chat. The Hearth is second; the lamp hardware is the cheapest
experiment on the page and starts in week one regardless. The understudy and
the council are third.

| Quarter | Work |
|---|---|
| Q1 | Sandbox, search, browser, permission ledger, replay |
| Q2 | Voice, transcription, participant voice, lamp hardware, CRDT reading |
| Q3 | Growing registry, wiki, understudy, continuous loop |
| Q4 | Real-money sleeve gate, lamp v2, time travel |

## Risks to keep

- Hands with a real credential can lose real things. Two keys, caps and replay
  reduce this; they do not remove it.
- The understudy can offend the person it imitates. Label plus review loop is
  the whole defence; kill it fast if either human flinches.
- This host serves production (somanotes, postgres, mongo). A sandbox on the
  same box is a blast-radius problem; the Workshop belongs on a second machine.
- A continuous loop at Fable/Opus prices is a monthly bill. Budget it as a line
  item before building it.
- Synchronous voice needs both free at once. If time zones are wrong, the lamp
  and voice notes carry the closeness, not live audio.
- If the September experiment showed human volume rising, the "pull is the
  limiter" hypothesis weakens and the Hearth moves ahead of the Workshop.

## Dissent recorded at writing

The page assumes the pair wants more hours in the house. If what they want is
better thinking in fewer hours, the wiki and time travel are the moonshot and
the Hearth is noise.

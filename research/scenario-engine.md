Cascade / Domino Modeling\n\n### Network Science: Cascading Failure Models\n\nThe academic literature on cascading failures comes from three main domains: power grid failures, financial contagion, and epidemic spreading. All three are directly applicable.\n\n**The load-redistribution model**: When a node in a network fails (a refinery goes offline, a bank defaults, a supply chain link breaks), its load redistributes to neighboring nodes. If any neighbor is already near capacity, the additional load causes it to fail too, creating a cascade. The math:\n\n- Each node *i* has a load *L_i* and a capacity *C_i*\n- When node *i* fails, its load distributes to neighbors proportional to their connectivity\n- If *L_j + redistributed_load > C_j*, node *j* fails\n- Repeat until no more failures occur\n\n**Key finding**: Systems with heterogeneous capacity distribution (some nodes much stronger than others) are more resilient than homogeneous systems. Applied to your cascade: the US economy has enormous buffers (SPR, fiscal capacity, diverse energy mix) while smaller EM economies don't. The cascade hits weaker nodes first.\n\n**The sandpile model / self-organized criticality** (Per Bak, 1987): A sandpile grows grain by grain. Most grains cause nothing. Occasionally one grain triggers an avalanche. The system self-organizes to a \"critical state\" where avalanches of all sizes are possible, following a power law distribution. The profound implication: **you cannot predict when the cascade will start, but you can measure how close the system is to criticality**.\n\nApplied to macro: when global spare oil capacity is high (5+ million bpd), the system is far from criticality — a supply disruption gets absorbed. When spare capacity is thin (1-2 million bpd, as in 2026), the system is at criticality — any disruption can cascade. **The indicator is the buffer, not the trigger.**\n\nMeasurable \"distance from criticality\" metrics:\n- OPEC spare capacity (physical oil buffer)\n- Strategic petroleum reserve levels (political buffer)\n- Global grain stocks-to-use ratio (food buffer)\n- EM sovereign forex reserves in months of imports (financial buffer)\n- Credit default swap spreads (market-implied stress)\n\n### Epidemic / Contagion Models as Economic Analogs\n\nThe SIR (Susceptible-Infected-Recovered) model maps surprisingly well to economic shock propagation:\n\n- **Susceptible** = sectors/economies exposed to the shock but not yet affected\n- **Infected** = sectors/economies actively experiencing the shock\n- **Recovered** = sectors/economies that have adapted (substitution, policy response)\n\nThe **basic reproduction number R₀** in epidemiology (how many new infections each infected person causes) has a direct analog: **the amplification factor** of an economic shock. This is solvable as a fixed-point problem (iterative algorithm, converges quickly).\n\n**The DebtRank model** (Battiston et al., 2012): Extends Eisenberg-Noe by measuring the *fraction of the network's value destroyed* by the failure of each node. Time-Based Triggers and Deadlines\n\n### Date-Gated Triggers\n\nThis is an underappreciated concept in scenario modeling. Most models focus on price/level triggers (\"if Brent > $130, then X\"). But some of the most important triggers are calendar-based:\n\n**Agricultural deadlines** (hard, physical, non-negotiable):\n- US corn planting window: roughly April 1 - May 15 (varies by state, but April 15 is the \"you should really be starting\" date for the central corn belt)\n- US soybean planting: May 1 - June 15\n- US winter wheat harvest: June-July\n- Brazil safrinha corn planting: January-March\n- Northern hemisphere fertilizer application: February-April\n\n**Financial deadlines**:\n- Quarter-end rebalancing (March 31, June 30, September 30, December 31)\n- Options expiration (monthly, quarterly)\n- Margin call deadlines (T+2 after call)\n- Bond coupon payment dates (for EM sovereign default scenarios)\n- IMF review dates (for countries under programs)\n\n**Political deadlines**:\n- Election dates\n- Sanction snap-back dates\n- Treaty expiration dates\n- Congressional authorization expiration\n\n### Modeling Irreversibility Thresholds\n\nThe concept of **irreversibility** is critical and often missing from scenario models. Some events, once they happen (or once a deadline passes), cannot be undone:\n\n**The framework**: Each cascade node can have a **persistence threshold** — a combination of severity and duration that makes the impact permanent:\n\n- \"If Hormuz is closed for <1 week, it's a blip. If closed for >3 weeks, pipeline contracts are renegotiated and the map changes permanently.\"\n- \"If diesel is above $6/gal for <2 weeks, truckers absorb it. If >6 weeks, small operators go bankrupt and capacity is destroyed.\"\n- \"If corn isn't planted by May 15, that crop is gone. No policy response can fix it.\"\n\n**Mathematical formulation**: Irreversibility can be modeled as a **path-dependent barrier**. It's not just \"price > threshold\" but \"price > threshold for duration > D.\" This is analogous to a **Parisian barrier option** in derivatives pricing:\n\n- **Instantaneous trigger**: fires when level is breached (standard barrier)\n- **Duration trigger**: fires when level has been breached for N consecutive days (Parisian barrier)\n- **Cumulative trigger**: fires when level has been above threshold for N total days (not necessarily consecutive)\n- **Calendar trigger**: fires on a specific date if a condition holds (the planting deadline model)\n- **Compound trigger**: fires when condition A AND condition B AND date > D (the most realistic model: \"if diesel is still above $5.50 AND we're past April 15 AND USDA planting progress is below 15%, then the planting miss cascade activates\")\n\n### Countdown and Window Triggers\n\n**Countdown trigger**: \"You have N days until this becomes irreversible.\" This is the most actionable format for a trader. Example: \"18 days until corn planting deadline. Current diesel: $5.80/gal. Planting progress: 3% (vs. 5-year avg 8%). Status: RED.\"\n\n**Window trigger**: \"The window for X is open from date A to date B.\" If the window closes without the condition being met, the cascade node activates. Example: \"OPEC+ emergency meeting window: next scheduled meeting June 1. If no emergency meeting called before April 30, no supply response until June.\"\n\n**Expiry trigger**: \"Policy X expires on date Y. If not renewed, impact Z.\" Example: \"US diesel fuel tax holiday expires July 31. If not renewed, effective diesel price increases $0.24/gal.\"\n\n### What's Implementable\n\nA trigger system where each trigger has:\n```\n{\n  \"id\": \"corn_planting_deadline\",\n  \"type\": \"compound\",\n  \"conditions\": [\n    {\"variable\": \"diesel_price\", \"operator\": \">\", \"threshold\": 5.50},\n    {\"variable\": \"planting_progress_pct\", \"operator\": \"<\", \"threshold\": 20},\n    {\"variable\": \"date\", \"operator\": \">\", \"value\": \"2026-04-15\"}\n  ],\n  \"logic\": \"all\",  // all conditions must be true\n  \"consequence\": \"planting_miss_cascade\",\n  \"irreversible\": true,\n  \"countdown_display\": true\n}\n```\n\nThis can be evaluated with simple Python logic.

## 1. Oil to Employment Pathway

### Hamilton Framework
- All but 1 of 11 postwar U.S. recessions preceded by oil price spike (~9-month lag)
- Oil shocks explain **20-25%** of cyclical employment variability (2x monetary policy)
- Job reallocation rose **11% of employment over 3-4 years** after 1973 shock (Davis & Haltiwanger)
- Mechanism: sectoral reallocation + consumer spending diversion, NOT uniform price rises
- Workers idle in depressed sectors rather than shifting -- persistent unemployment overhang

### 2008 Template (Primary Analog)
- Jan 2008: oil ~$90 | Jul 11 peak: $147 | Dec 2008 bottom: $32
- Lag from peak to catastrophic job losses: **~3 months** (Jul peak -> Oct 240K/mo losses)
- Lag from initial acceleration to recession declaration: **~9 months**
- Oct 2008 - Mar 2009 average: **-712,000 jobs/month**
- U.S. petroleum demand fell **1.2M bbl/day (-5.8%)** in 2008, largest since 1980
- SUV sales plunged >25% YoY by May-Jun 2008

### Diesel Transmission (Current, Mar 2026)
- U.S. diesel: **$5.38/gal** (+41% since war began), 2nd time ever above $5
- Diesel crack spread: $30 -> **$70/bbl** (downstream pain exceeds headline crude)
- Semi fuel cost: **$0.90/mile** (up from $0.60), wiping out typical $0.05/mile margin
- Fuel surcharges recover only 60-70% of increase; carriers absorb the rest
- Demand destruction thresholds: $4.75 = GDP drag, $5.00 = destruction begins, $5.25 = recession prob >40%
- **Currently above all three thresholds**

### Leading Indicators (Current)
- U.S. PMI employment sub-index: **first decline in over a year** (Mar 2026)
- Input prices: 10-month high; output prices: 3.5-year high
- Supplier delivery times: worst since Oct 2022
- Australia composite PMI collapsed 52.4 -> **47.0** (contraction) -- canary

### Key Papers
- Hamilton, "Oil and the Macroeconomy since WWII" (1983, JPE)
- Hamilton, "What Is an Oil Shock?" (2003)
- Hamilton, "Causes and Consequences of the Oil Shock of 2007-08" (Brookings, 2009)
- Davis & Haltiwanger, "Sectoral Job Creation and Destruction Responses to Oil Price Changes" (2001)

## 2. Fertilizer to Food Pathway

### Hormuz Fertilizer Exposure
- **20-30%** of global fertilizer exports transit Hormuz (urea, ammonia, phosphates, sulfur)
- **50%** of global urea and sulfur exports transit Hormuz
- Urea: Gulf = ~43% of seaborne exports; **17.1%** of U.S. consumption from Gulf sources
- Ammonia: ~25% globally traded from Gulf; Asia imports **64%** from Middle East
- Phosphate: Gulf = ~20% of global supply; **45%** of global sulfur (needed to process phosphate rock)
- Potash: 0% Hormuz exposure (Canada, Russia, Belarus routes)

### NOLA Urea Prices (Calibration Points)
- Pre-war (Feb 27): ~$475-516/mt
- Mar 5: $683/mt (+32% in one week)
- Mar 19: barge prices "well into $690s/t FOB NOLA"
- Mar 25 DTN retail: **$822.50/t** (+39% from prior report)
- Egypt FOB granular: ~$700/mt (up from $400-490 pre-war)
- Middle East spot: +50% in late Feb/early March

### Planting Window Mechanics
- **Corn Belt window: mid-April to mid-May** (southern opens ~2 weeks earlier)
- Nitrogen typically ordered March, applied April-May
- Late March status: U.S. ~25% short of usual spring supplies
- Yield penalty for late planting: **1-2 bushels/acre/day** outside optimal window
- Sidedress fallback: delay to V6-V8 (late May/June) IF 20+ lbs/ac starter applied at planting
- **April 15 deadline**: if Hormuz not reopened, corn belt nitrogen structurally impaired

### Farmer Decision Matrix (if Hormuz stays closed past mid-April)
1. Apply at elevated prices -> margin destruction
2. Reduce application rates -> **10-30% yield loss on corn**
3. Switch to soybeans -> reduces national corn production

### Transmission Timeline
- Weeks 1-4: wholesale fertilizer spike, futures react
- 1-3 months: retail catches up, planting decisions altered
- 6-12 months: reduced yields at harvest (Sep-Nov for U.S. corn)
- 9-18 months: consumer food prices absorb shock

### 2022 Precedent
- Despite 2021-22 fertilizer spikes, demand fell only modestly (high crop prices offset)
- 2026 difference: crop prices NOT elevated, so farmers face unhedged margin squeeze

## 3. EM Vulnerability

### Tier 1 (Highest Risk -- simultaneous oil + food + funding shock)

**Egypt**
- Pre-war 5Y CDS: ~270 bps; widened +44 bps (EMBI); 2023-24 crisis hit ~1,800 bps
- Energy import bill: $560M/mo -> **$1.65B/mo** (+$1.1B/mo)
- Each $10/bbl oil increase worsens current account by ~$2.5B
- Total external balance deterioration: ~$8B
- Hot money outflows: $1.4B in 8 days; projected $3-5B total
- Suez Canal losses: ~$10B (World Bank)
- Food & Beverages: **32%** of CPI basket
- Fuel price hikes: 15-30% in petrol, diesel, cooking gas

**Pakistan**
- Imports **80%** of energy from Gulf
- KSE-100 dropped 9.57% on Mar 2
- Schools closed, 4-day work week imposed
- Wheat harvest (Apr-May) at record input cost

**Bangladesh**
- Imports **95%** of oil; **65%** of LNG from Qatar (force majeure declared)
- Fuel reserves "projected to deplete within days"

### Tier 2
- **Sri Lanka**: 60% energy imports, post-2022 default
- **Philippines**: 98% of oil from Middle East, declared national energy emergency
- **Tunisia, Jordan, Senegal, Angola, Ethiopia, Zambia**: also flagged

### EM Aggregate Signals
- EMBI spread widening: +17 bps to 268 bps (aggregate)
- EM bond issuance: effectively frozen
- Capital outflows: $3.3B from EM debt in week to Mar 19
- $5B+ from HY corporate bonds (biggest since Apr 2025 tariff shock)

### Food CPI Weight (Vulnerability Metric)
- >50%: Nigeria, Kenya, Myanmar, Bangladesh

- 30-40%: Egypt (32%), Kenya (33%)
- <15%: U.S. (10.4%), UK, Australia

## 4. Dollar Squeeze Dynamics

- DXY ~99.65, rebounded 5% from four-year lows; testing 100-100.50 resistance
- Strengthening from **dollar scarcity**, not U.S. economic resilience
- Oil importers need more dollars to sustain demand -> structural shortage
- USD/ZAR +2.66% YTD; USD/INR +4.22% YTD
- Rising DXY tightens conditions for EM dollar-denominated debt (eurodollar feedback loop)
- Money market fund assets hit record **$7.86 trillion** (cash flight)
- Fed delayed rate cuts: oil-driven inflation prevents easing while dollar demand surges
- **Paradox**: worse oil shock -> stronger dollar -> tighter funding -> more demand destruction

- 2008: ~3M bbl/day at risk; 2026: **~20M bbl/day** transit blocked (7x)
- 2008: financial system amplified; 2026: physical shortage + fertilizer cascade + EM sovereign risk
- Hamilton's 9-month lag from late Feb 2026 start -> severe employment effects **Q4 2026 / Q1 2027**
- HBR analysis: supply chain cost transmission hits in **1-3 months** (May-Jun 2026)
